Abstract
Data centers have become essential infrastructure for digital services, while their rapidly
growing electricity demand makes coordinated workload and power management an im-
portant optimization problem. This paper studies the multi-data-center operation problem
under time-of-use electricity pricing and formulates it as a multi-data-center mixed-integer
nonlinear programming model (MDC-MINLP). The model jointly represents binary task
scheduling decisions, including temporal workload shifting and spatial task migration,
and continuous power-side variables, including device-level utilization, IT and auxiliary
power consumption, energy storage dynamics, grid power procurement, and quality-of-
service constraints. The objective is to minimize the total operating cost by integrating
electricity purchasing cost, IT operation loss, storage degradation cost, and migration cost.
To solve the resulting large-scale discrete–continuous coupled problem, an Adaptive Large
Neighborhood Search algorithm with Hierarchical Collaboration (HC-ALNS) is proposed.
HC-ALNS reconstructs feasible task action sets, employs a surrogate objective for fast
candidate screening, performs accurate power-layer evaluation for selected solutions, and
adaptively adjusts search intensity according to convergence behavior. Numerical results
show that HC-ALNS reduces the total operating cost by 3.67% and achieves better conver-
gence and solution quality than NSGA-II and PSO. These findings demonstrate that the
proposed MDC-MINLP and HC-ALNS provide an effective mathematical optimization
framework for coordinated computation–power scheduling.
Keywords: mixed-integer nonlinear programming; adaptive large neighborhood search;
hierarchical optimization; discrete–continuous optimization; multi-data-center scheduling;
computation–power coordination

1. Introduction
With the rapid development of cloud computing, artificial intelligence, and big data
technologies, data centers—which are critical to digital economic infrastructure—have ex-
perienced continuous growth in both scale and computing demand, leading to increasingly
prominent energy consumption issues. Studies indicate that the global electricity consump-
tion of data centers has reached approximately 415 TWh, accounting for about 1.5% of

global electricity demand, with an average annual growth rate of around 12% in recent
years [1]. Due to the increasing prevalence of computation-intensive applications such as
artificial intelligence, this figure is projected to increase to approximately 945 TWh by 2030,
accounting for nearly 3% of global electricity demand [ 2,3]. Meanwhile, as data centers
continue to expand in scale, their high-power-density operation further increases instanta-
neous power demand, not only intensifying pressure on power systems but also raising the
complexity of operational decision-making [4]. Therefore, improving data center energy
efficiency is not only an engineering issue but also an important mathematical optimization
problem involving workload scheduling, resource allocation, and power-side coordination.
Extensive studies have been conducted on computing–power coordinated schedul-
ing for data centers. Unlike conventional high-energy-consuming loads, data centers
can achieve rapid power load shifting and regulation through the temporal and spa-
tial migration of computing workloads, thereby providing flexible decision variables for
mathematical scheduling models. The authors of [5] classify delay-tolerant workloads as
short-delay, long-duration continuous, and interruptible types, and establish correspond-
ing temporal shifting models, which are embedded into day-ahead optimal scheduling
of data center microgrids to reduce energy consumption and curtailment costs. Mean-
while, in [ 6], the authors propose a spatiotemporal load-shifting framework based on
virtual links, transforming data center load flexibility into dispatchable resources that can
participate in electricity market clearing while explicitly defining pricing and compensa-
tion mechanisms. Local flexibility market mechanisms provide further support for the
market-oriented utilization of load flexibility. Reference [7] provides a systematic review
of the modeling and clearing methods of local flexibility markets and establishes a multi-
agent coordinated trading framework. The authors of [ 8] develop a multi-data-center
energy consumption model that considers spatiotemporal flexibility; jointly characterizes
delay-tolerant task scheduling, IT equipment operation, and cooling processes; and defines
an energy flexibility region to describe the feasible power consumption space. Further-
more, in [9], the authors transform the internal operational constraints of data centers into
privacy-preserving flexibility region constraints, enabling the reliability of power systems
to be assessed without overlooking load flexibility. These studies show that the flexible
operation of data centers can be mathematically represented through schedulable regions,
coupling constraints, and optimization models. However, most existing studies still focus
on macroscopic load flexibility or regional flexibility representations, while the fine-grained
coupling between task-level scheduling decisions and power-side operational variables
remains insufficiently explored.
Scheduling problems under time-varying electricity prices have increasingly been
studied from the perspective of mathematical optimization. Feng et al. [ 10] formulate
an energy-efficient batch scheduling problem under time-of-use electricity prices as a
mixed-integer linear programming model and solve it using heuristic algorithms. Zhao
and Zhu [11] develop a chance-constrained stochastic mixed-integer linear programming
model and a reinforcement-learning-based adaptive large neighborhood search algorithm
for continuous-time energy-aware scheduling. These studies show that mathematical
programming and improved heuristics are effective tools for solving large-scale scheduling
problems with coupled decisions.
Although refined mathematical models can accurately capture the coupling char-
acteristics of computing–power coordination, they usually introduce a large number of
integer variables, nonlinear relationships, and strongly coupled constraints, resulting in
high computational complexity and low solution efficiency. Therefore, many researchers
have proposed metaheuristic and hybrid optimization methods to improve scheduling effi-
ciency. Attiya et al. [12] propose an intelligent chimp optimizer combined with the marine

predators algorithm and a disruption operator for IoT task scheduling in fog computing,
showing the potential of hybrid metaheuristics for large-scale task allocation problems.
The authors of [13] propose a hybrid algorithm that combines the genetic algorithm (GA)
with particle swarm optimization (PSO), where GA enhances population diversity through
crossover and mutation, mitigating the premature convergence of PSO and improving
both global search capability and convergence speed. In [ 14], the researchers develop a
discrete non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm (DNSGA-II) for fog–cloud collabora-
tive multi-objective scheduling by redesigning crossover and mutation operators to adapt
to task assignment decisions. The authors of [ 15] propose an improved PSO combined
with simulated annealing (IPSO-SA), incorporating Tent chaotic mapping for initializa-
tion and nonlinear adaptive inertia weights, along with the Metropolis criterion to avoid
premature convergence. The study in [16] designs a load–energy–time-aware ant colony
optimization (LET-ACO) algorithm for dynamic energy management in cloud data centers,
integrating dynamic voltage scaling and load-balancing mechanisms to reduce energy
consumption without affecting system performance. Other optimization algorithms, such
as artificial bee colony [17], genetic optimization [18], cuckoo search [19], and chaotic cat
swarm optimization [20], have also been widely studied. These methods provide important
algorithmic foundations for solving large-scale scheduling problems, but most of them
are general-purpose heuristics and do not fully exploit the hierarchical coupling structure
between task-level scheduling and power-side operation. Therefore, a problem-oriented
algorithmic framework is still needed for large-scale data center scheduling problems with
discrete–continuous coupling.
Although significant progress has been achieved in computing–power coordinated
optimization, several limitations remain:
1. Insufficient task-level mathematical modeling: Existing studies are mostly based on
macroscopic flexible load representations and lack a unified task-level formulation
of temporal flexibility, spatial migration capability, and queue evolution. This makes
it difficult to capture the combinatorial decision structure and fine-grained dynamic
characteristics of heterogeneous workloads during scheduling.
2. Incomplete discrete–continuous coupling between computing and power systems:
Most methods address computing scheduling and power operation separately, failing
to jointly model the coupling among computing utilization, IT equipment power
consumption, cooling demand, power distribution losses, energy storage operation,
and task migration. As a result, the resulting optimization model may not fully
describe the actual interaction between binary scheduling decisions and continuous
power-side variables.
3. Lack of tailored solution methods for strongly coupled optimization: Due to the
strong coupling between discrete task scheduling and continuous power decisions,
existing methods mainly rely on general-purpose heuristics. These methods are
not specifically designed for the hybrid mathematical structure of multi-data-center
coordinated operation and often suffer from large search spaces, high evaluation costs,
and unsatisfactory convergence performance.
To address the above issues, this paper formulates a multi-data-center mixed-integer
nonlinear programming model (MDC-MINLP) for task-level computation–power coordi-
nated scheduling and proposes an Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search algorithm with
Hierarchical Collaboration (HC-ALNS). The proposed model provides a unified mathe-
matical formulation for task spatiotemporal migration, device-level computing utilization,
flexible queue evolution, power consumption, energy storage operation, and quality-of-
service constraints. The proposed algorithm further exploits the hierarchical structure of

accurate power-layer evaluation, and adaptive search intensity adjustment.
The main contributions of this paper are as follows:
• A task-level mathematical scheduling model is proposed to describe the dynamic
scheduling characteristics of multi-type workloads. The model explicitly represents
temporal shiftability, spatial migration capability, flexible queue evolution, device-
level computing capacity constraints, and quality-of-service requirements, thereby
transforming heterogeneous workload management into a structured mixed-integer
optimization problem.
• A computation–power coupled optimization model is formulated for multi-data-center
coordinated operation. Aiming to minimize the total system operating cost, the model
jointly considers task scheduling constraints, computing capacity constraints, power
balance constraints, IT and auxiliary power consumption, energy storage operation,
and migration cost. By integrating electricity purchasing cost, IT operation loss, storage
degradation cost, and migration cost into a unified objective function, the proposed
MDC-MINLP captures the discrete–continuous coupling between workload allocation
and power-side operation.
• An Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search algorithm with Hierarchical Collaboration
(HC-ALNS) is proposed for the resulting large-scale hybrid optimization problem. The
algorithm reduces combinatorial complexity through task action-space reconstruction,
accelerates candidate solution screening using a surrogate objective, performs accurate
power-layer evaluation for selected solutions, and adaptively adjusts search intensity
according to convergence behavior. This design improves the efficiency and solution
quality of the optimization process while maintaining consistency with the real total
cost objective.
This study is organized into three main parts. First, a multi-data-center mixed-integer
nonlinear programming model for task-level computation–power coordinated scheduling
is formulated, and an Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search algorithm with Hierarchical
Collaboration is developed. Second, the performance of the proposed method is evaluated
through numerical experiments. Finally, the main findings are summarized and conclusions
are drawn.
2. Materials and Methods
This section presents the scheduling framework, formulates a multi-data-center mixed-
integer nonlinear programming model (MDC-MINLP) for task-level computation–power
coordinated scheduling, and constructs the total system operating cost model. An Adaptive
Large Neighborhood Search algorithm with Hierarchical Collaboration is then developed
to solve the resulting problem.
2.1. Scheduling Framework
The overall framework of the studied system is illustrated in Figure 1. Traditional
studies on data centers usually focus on real-time workload processing, whereas this paper
models multiple data centers as integrated entities with schedulable workload resources
and controllable power-side resources. During normal operation, data centers receive
user workloads and execute them using computing devices supplied by the power grid,
while cooling and power distribution systems provide auxiliary support. Therefore, the
operational state of a data center is determined not only by the amount of workload it
receives but also by task scheduling decisions, resource utilization levels, and corresponding
power consumption.
https://doi.org/10.3390/math14122225

--- Page 5 ---
Mathematics 2026, 14, 2225 5 of 29
Figure 1. Framework of the coordinated operation.
Specifically, the multi-data-center system is represented as a three-layer framework
consisting of the task-scheduling layer, the data center operation layer, and the power
support layer. At the task-scheduling layer, workload flexibility is modeled through
feasible temporal and spatial scheduling decisions. Some tasks can be shifted to different
time slots, some can be migrated among data centers, and some possess both types of
flexibility. These decisions determine the task execution time and location, and thus define
the workload distribution over the scheduling horizon. At the data center operation layer,
task scheduling results are mapped into device-level computing loads and utilization levels,
which further determine IT power consumption and auxiliary power demand. At the power
support layer, energy storage charging and discharging decisions are coordinated with
time-of-use (TOU) electricity prices and grid procurement, providing additional degrees of
freedom for cost optimization.
Therefore, the three layers are not independent modules but are linked by mathemati-
cal mappings and coupling constraints. Task scheduling decisions determine computing
load and utilization; utilization determines IT, cooling, and distribution power consump-
tion; and power demand further affects grid purchase and storage operation. Conversely,
electricity price parameters and storage constraints influence the optimal spatiotemporal
allocation of workloads through the total cost objective. This bidirectional relationship
forms a closed-loop computation–power coordinated scheduling mechanism.
These interactions indicate the necessity of establishing a unified MDC-MINLP for
multi-data-center operation. Such a model should jointly describe task scheduling con-
straints, computing capacity constraints, power balance constraints, storage operation
constraints, and quality-of-service requirements, thereby supporting coordinated optimiza-
tion of workload allocation and power-side operation.

2.3. Solution Algorithm
The multi-data-center collaborative scheduling problem involves both discrete deci-
sions, such as task execution location and start time, and continuous decisions, such as grid
purchasing power, energy storage power, and state of charge. Therefore, it is a discrete–
continuous coupled hybrid optimization problem. To improve the solution efficiency in
large-scale scenarios, an Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search algorithm with Hierarchical
Collaboration (HC-ALNS) is developed. The algorithm iteratively solves the real total cost
minimization problem through “task-layer discrete reconstruction—power-layer accurate
evaluation—adaptive search intensity adjustment”.

3.2. Scenario Settings
Based on the proposed computing–power coordinated scheduling model, multiple oper-
ational scenarios are designed to evaluate the effectiveness of different scheduling strategies:
1. Scenario 1 (Baseline): No scheduling optimization is applied. All tasks are executed at
their original arrival times and within their originating data centers.
2. Scenario 2 (Time-only scheduling): Only temporal flexibility is considered. Tasks are
allowed to shift across time slots, but cross-data-center migration is not permitted.
3. Scenario 3 (Space-only scheduling): Only spatial flexibility is considered. Tasks can
migrate across data centers while their execution time remains unchanged.
4. Scenario 4 (Spatiotemporal scheduling): Both temporal and spatial flexibility are
considered. Tasks can be shifted across time slots and migrated across data centers.
5. Scenario 5 (Spatiotemporal scheduling with energy storage): Based on Scenario 4,
energy storage is further integrated into system operation.

3.3. Model Effectiveness Analysis
The total system cost after optimization and the corresponding convergence processes for
Scenarios 1–5 are illustrated in Figure 6. All scenarios converge to stable objective values after
49 iterations, while the final costs differ significantly under different scheduling strategies.
Figure 6. Cost iteration curves under five scenarios.
Specifically , the space-only scheduling scenario achieves a final cost of 1,076,638.32 CNY ,
corresponding to 0.51% reduction. The time-only scheduling scenario reduces the cost
to 1,058,636.49 CNY, with a 2.18% reduction. With the integration of both temporal and
spatial scheduling, the cost decreases further to 1,056,563.15 CNY, achieving a reduction
of 2.37%. When energy storage is incorporated, the system cost is further reduced to
1,042,436.52 CNY, corresponding to a total reduction of 3.67%. A cross-scenario comparison
reveals that space-only scheduling provides limited cost improvement because it merely
redistributes workloads among data centers without altering their allocation across high-
and low-price periods, showing limited ability to reduce high-cost electricity consumption.
In contrast, time-only scheduling directly adjusts task execution timing, shifting workloads
from high- to low-price periods, which significantly reduces electricity procurement costs
and serves as the primary source of cost savings. When spatial scheduling is incorporated
to a greater extent, additional cost reduction is achieved. This indicates that cross-data-
center workload redistribution can further optimize electricity price matching within the
same time period, allowing workloads to be allocated to data centers with more favorable
electricity prices or greater capacity, thereby creating additional savings potential. With
the integration of energy storage, the system cost decreases further to 1,042,436.52 CNY.
In this case, the system not only avoids high-price periods through task scheduling but
also leverages energy storage to charge during low-price periods and discharge during
high-price periods. As a result, the reduction in electricity procurement cost is no longer
solely dependent on workload migration, but is achieved through coordinated optimization
of both computing and power resources.

3.4. Algorithm Effectiveness Analysis
To validate the effectiveness of the proposed method, NSGA-II, PSO, and the proposed
HC-ALNS algorithm are applied to solve Scenario 5. The corresponding cost convergence
curves are presented in Figure 9. In addition to the convergence curves, the wall-clock time
required by each algorithm to reach the stopping iteration is also recorded to evaluate the
computational overhead.
Figure 9. Cost iteration comparison of different algorithms.
As shown in the aforementioned figure, all three algorithms achieve a rapid cost
reduction during the initial stage; however, their subsequent convergence behaviors differ
significantly. The relevant iteration data are presented in Table 2. Specifically, PSO enters a
plateau phase relatively early after an initial decrease, with limited further improvement,
indicating a tendency toward premature convergence. Although PSO requires the shortest
wall-clock time of 275.9 s, its final cost is 1,055,640 CNY, corresponding to a cost reduction
of only 2.45%. In contrast, NSGA-II continues to exhibit gradual improvement in the
mid-to-late stages, although its overall convergence speed remains relatively slow. This
is also reflected in its wall-clock time of 1220.1 s, which is the longest among the three
algorithms, while its final cost reduction is 2.74%. Compared with these methods, the
HC-ALNS algorithm not only achieves a substantial cost reduction in the early stage but
also maintains continuous improvement in solution quality during subsequent iterations.
Although it reaches the optimal solution at a relatively later stage, this behavior suggests
that the algorithm is less prone to being trapped in local optima and possesses stronger

3.5. Sensitivity and Robustness Analysis
To further characterize the impact mechanisms of key parameters with regard to
system performance, this subsection conducts a sensitivity analysis focusing on the pro-
portion of transferable workloads and the degree of electricity price fluctuation. For data
centers, transferable workloads serve as the primary carrier for peak shaving, valley filling,
and cross-data-center coordinated scheduling, while electricity price variation provides
a direct economic incentive for workload migration. Therefore, under the premise of a
fixed total computational workload, the effects of these two factors on cost reduction are
systematically analyzed by adjusting the transferable workload factor and the electricity
price fluctuation factor.
Both parameters are varied within the range of [ −0.2, 0.2]. As shown in Figure 12,
the cost reduction generally exhibits a monotonic trend with respect to both factors, with
the electricity price fluctuation factor exerting a more pronounced influence. As the de-
gree of price fluctuation increases, the peak–valley price gap widens. This allows the
scheduling strategy to more effectively exploit price signals and shift workloads toward
low-price periods, thereby significantly enhancing the system’s cost-saving potential. Over-
all, the cost reduction shows an approximately linear increase with respect to the electricity
price fluctuation factor, indicating that the pricing mechanism plays a dominant role in
driving optimization benefits. In contrast, the impact of the transferable workload factor
demonstrates a clear diminishing marginal effect. At relatively low levels of transferable
workload, increasing its proportion enhances scheduling flexibility and yields noticeable
cost reductions. However, as the proportion continues to grow, the improvement gradually
saturates due to system capacity constraints and the inherent temporal–spatial distribution
of workloads, reducing the sensitivity to further changes in this parameter. Moreover, when
the proportion of transferable workload decreases, the system cost initially experiences a
slight reduction and then enters a plateau phase. This suggests that the proposed model can
adaptively adjust scheduling strategies under different workload compositions to maintain

of workload structure on system cost diminishes.

4. Discussion
This paper addresses the mathematical optimization problem arising from the coupled
scheduling of computational workloads and power-side operation in multi-data-center
systems. A multi-data-center mixed-integer nonlinear programming model (MDC-MINLP)
is formulated for task-level computation–power coordinated scheduling, in which binary
task scheduling decisions, device-level computing utilization, nonlinear power consump-
tion characteristics, energy storage dynamics, grid procurement, and quality-of-service
constraints are jointly represented. To solve the resulting large-scale discrete–continuous
coupled problem, an Adaptive Large Neighborhood Search algorithm with Hierarchical
Collaboration (HC-ALNS) is developed. The main findings are summarized as follows:
• By explicitly modeling task temporal flexibility, spatial migration capability, and
flexible queue constraints, the proposed MDC-MINLP provides a fine-grained feasible
region for workload restructuring. The optimization results show that task execution
decisions are no longer distributed only according to original arrival patterns, but are
reorganized according to electricity price signals and system constraints. Consequently,
workloads are reduced during high-price periods and reassigned to low-price periods,
indicating that the model can effectively transform task flexibility into cost-oriented
scheduling decisions.
• The comparison among different scheduling scenarios verifies the contribution of each
decision dimension to the objective value. Temporal scheduling provides the primary
reduction in total operating cost by shifting flexible workloads away from high-price
periods, achieving a cost reduction of 2.18%. On this basis, spatial scheduling further
expands the feasible solution space by enabling cross-data-center workload redistribu-
tion, increasing the reduction to 2.37%. When energy storage operation is incorporated,
https://doi.org/10.3390/math14122225

--- Page 26 ---
Mathematics 2026, 14, 2225 26 of 29
the model further couples workload allocation with charge–discharge decisions, using
the “charge at low price and discharge at high price” mechanism to improve the
power-side cost structure. As a result, the total cost reduction reaches 3.67%, demon-
strating that the joint optimization of task scheduling and power operation yields a
stronger objective improvement than single-dimensional scheduling.
• The proposed HC-ALNS algorithm shows better suitability for the structure of the
MDC-MINLP than general-purpose metaheuristics. Through task action-space recon-
struction, surrogate objective screening, accurate power-layer evaluation, and adaptive
search intensity adjustment, HC-ALNS maintains continuous improvement in later
iterations and reduces the probability of premature convergence. Compared with
NSGA-II and PSO, HC-ALNS achieves additional cost reductions of 0.93% and 1.22%,
respectively, indicating stronger convergence performance and higher final solution
quality for the studied discrete–continuous coupled optimization problem.
Furthermore, the sensitivity analysis provides insight into the influence of key param-
eters on the optimal objective value. The electricity price fluctuation factor has a significant
positive effect on cost reduction, indicating that the time-varying price coefficient is a
dominant parameter driving the optimization benefit. In contrast, the proportion of trans-
ferable workloads exhibits diminishing marginal returns. When the amount of schedulable
workload reaches a certain level, further increasing task flexibility only slightly enlarges the
effective feasible region and contributes less to additional cost reduction. Under different
parameter perturbations, the proposed MDC-MINLP and HC-ALNS maintain stable opti-
mization performance, demonstrating robustness with respect to changes in price signals
and workload flexibility.
These results indicate that the proposed mathematical modeling and solution frame-
work can effectively describe and solve the coordinated operation problem of multiple data
centers under time-of-use electricity pricing. By integrating task scheduling constraints,
nonlinear power consumption relationships, energy storage dynamics, and operating cost
components into a unified MDC-MINLP , the model captures the essential coupling between
computation-side and power-side decisions. Meanwhile, HC-ALNS provides an efficient
problem-oriented heuristic for obtaining high-quality feasible solutions in a large-scale
mixed-integer nonlinear search space.
Future research may further extend the proposed model by incorporating renewable
energy generation, uncertainty in workload arrivals and electricity prices, and real-time
rolling optimization mechanisms. In addition, the scalability of the proposed method for
larger networked data center systems, as well as its integration with electricity market
participation and stochastic optimization frameworks, remains an important direction for
further study.
